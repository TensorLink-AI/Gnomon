# v0.8 A1 calibration/action decision

Decision: promote the additive action-tier and calibration-lineage contract;
do not graduate reversible automation from ordinary forecast artifacts.

## Product result

Host policies may now distinguish `advisory`, `reversible_low_impact`, and
`high_impact`. Advisory and high-impact requests never actuate. A reversible
request must pass the existing policy, authority, selected-path, and support
gates plus typed checks for the exact artifact, series, selected model,
horizon, emitted nominal interval, held-out point count, measured coverage,
strict non-pooled residual split, cutoff, policy support floor, and prospective
validation status. The full signed receipt and compact agent response expose
the bindings, every check, and every failed check.

The contract does not alter a point forecast or interval. Adaptive tracking
state remains diagnostic and cannot authorize an action. Omitted action tiers
retain the legacy automation object and behavior exactly. The MCP channel
remains untrusted for authorization.

Current rolling-evaluation evidence does not claim a passed prospective
validation status. Consequently, an ordinary forecast can explain why a
reversible request is blocked but cannot enable it. A future governed host
evidence pipeline may supply `passed` only after the frozen prospective gates
are independently satisfied.

## Validation

The frozen 12-case policy matrix moved from 0/12 completed before the new
boundary to 12/12 complete, exact, sealed, and primary-preserving. Wrong
artifact/series/model/horizon lineage, pooled residuals, insufficient points,
out-of-band coverage, untrusted authority, advisory tier, and high-impact tier
all fail closed. A fully matched fixture additionally carries an explicit
passed prospective-validation status and demonstrates the bounded contract;
ordinary product artifacts do not manufacture that status.

The real tool-boundary probe found and fixed a general projection defect: the
compact agent response initially omitted calibration lineage that was present
in the signed receipt. Compact and full responses now agree. JSON round-trip
testing also found that semantic verification incorrectly depended on nested
dictionary insertion order; failed-check verification is now order-independent.

A sequential, resumable 120-case development shard used untouched futures
across level, trend, seasonal, intermittent, heteroskedastic, and regime-shift
families; horizons 3 and 12; and daily and hourly frequencies. Point forecasts
were identical between pooled and strict-split arms, and pooled calibration
never became policy-eligible. Strict-split behavior did not clear promotion:

- empirical 80% interval coverage was 67.37% versus 73.89% pooled;
- intermittent coverage was 36.11% and heteroskedastic coverage was 62.67%;
- mean interval score was 9.490 versus 7.740 pooled;
- mean three-quantile pinball loss was 0.632 versus 0.559 pooled; and
- 10 eligible direction actions were triggered and all 10 were correct, but
  that selective positive result does not override the calibration failures.

The untouched confirmation seed remains unconsumed because the frozen
development gates already failed. This is a safe disclosure improvement, not
evidence of calibrated automation or broad forecast-accuracy uplift.
