# Gnomon v0.7 forecast-quality loop

Status: complete with a release recommendation. The durable handoff is
`results/benchmark-releases/2026-08-30-v07-loop-i013/checkpoint.json`.

## Objective

Improve the forecasts and temporal conclusions agents can safely use, while
keeping Gnomon's evidence boundary legible to humans. The loop addresses five
observed gaps one bounded experiment at a time. A priority is complete only
when a general production change clears its frozen user gate, or when the
candidate family reaches a retained evidence-backed no-build decision.

The v0.6 release remains the stable comparison point. This branch does not
change v0.6 artifacts or PR #83.

## User outcomes

Every promoted change must improve at least one of these outcomes without
materially regressing the others:

- agents receive forecasts that beat an assumption-minimal fallback often
  enough to be useful, without novelty being mistaken for skill;
- agents preserve typed temporal facts, relationships, support, and
  abstentions when turning engine output into a final answer;
- humans can tell what changed the forecast, how strong the evidence is, and
  whether the result is safe for automation;
- admissible context is found often enough to help, while leakage and invented
  numerical effects remain zero;
- repeated runs choose stable mechanisms and finish within predictable call,
  token, retry, and latency budgets.

## Iteration cycle

Only one issue is active at a time.

1. Re-rank open issues from observed user harm and dependency order.
2. State the agent/human hypothesis and the behavior that must not regress.
3. Freeze corpus, arms, denominators, metrics, seeds, uncertainty, retry and
   timeout policy, promotion gate, and kill criterion before implementation.
4. Reproduce the gap on the smallest representative shard and retain raw
   responses, engine artifacts, manifests, completeness, commit, and dirty
   state.
5. Make one general production-useful change. Benchmark names, task IDs,
   labels, answer choices, source identities, and hidden futures may never be
   runtime inputs.
6. Add contract, property, and negative-path tests and run the focused suite.
7. Run a matched independent treatment/control validation. API work uses
   `jobs=1`, incremental rows, resumable checkpoints, bounded retries, per-call
   timeouts, and a wall-clock bound. Missing rows stay in denominators.
8. Inspect actual engine artifacts and final agent responses. Engine-contract
   completeness and agent preservation are separate results.
9. Decide `promote`, `revise`, or `reject` against the frozen gate. Rejected
   mechanisms remain documented and disabled or removed.
10. Write a checkpoint containing results, evidence paths, residual risks, and
    the exact next action; commit only intended files; continue from that file.

## Invariants

These are exact gates in every iteration:

- no future leakage or benchmark-identity routing;
- the immutable primary forecast remains byte-for-byte unchanged by context,
  question answering, or agent synthesis unless a separately governed
  forecast-selection policy explicitly admits a replacement;
- support, provenance, warnings, and automation eligibility are never upgraded
  by presentation code;
- typed `no_distinct_numeric_path` and other relationship-valued answers are
  preserved exactly through the agent boundary;
- raw artifacts, denominators, retry exhaustion, fallbacks, and revisions are
  retained rather than silently replaced;
- pre-existing committed work, benchmark artifacts, and untracked files are
  preserved. `docs/astrid-btc-agent-plan.md` is outside this work and is never
  added to a v0.7 commit.

## Issue register and frozen completion gates

External evaluation findings from the published 0.5.0 wheel are integrated
through `docs/v0.7-external-evaluation-intake.md`. They are reproduced on the
current exact head before implementation. Decision-input integrity (S1) and
bounded timestamp jitter (S2) are safety pre-emption checks after the current
Q1 benchmark baseline and before its first production change. Forecast,
typed-answer, anomaly, MCP, routing, and polish findings are absorbed by the
owning issue so two iterations never edit or validate the same boundary in
parallel.

### Q1 — Useful departures from last-value

Observed gap: in the v0.6 80-row TemporalBench run, 476 of 480 submitted
channel paths equalled last-value. The four departures were two duplicated
T2/T4 views of `seasonal_naive` selections and all four scored worse than
last-value. This diagnoses an admission problem; those labels cannot be used
to tune the fix.

User hypothesis: agents should receive a non-flat path when prefix-only
evidence shows repeatable skill, while humans should retain the safe fallback
when that evidence is weak.

The first independent development screen is frozen at seed `92741`. It must
exercise the production selector on 40 cases each from true seasonal,
phase-unstable/pseudo-seasonal, trend, level, and intermittent families, with
an untouched final horizon. Half of each family uses horizon 3 with histories
drawn from 15, 18, 21, and 24 observations; half uses horizon 12 with histories
drawn from 42, 45, and 47 observations. Seasonal families declare period 6;
the others declare period 1. Previously scored TemporalBench and CiK rows are
regression-only after the mechanism is frozen. Existing panel pooling is
measured before another model family is added. Toto2-4M remains a no-build
unless new independent supply evidence exists; its old gate is not loosened.

The paired effect is bounded relative MAE gain,
`(baseline - candidate) / max(baseline, candidate)`, so an all-zero future
cannot create an unbounded ratio. Promotion requires at least 99% completion,
no silent fallback, positive paired median gain among admitted departures,
more wins than losses among departures, a non-negative deterministic 90%
paired-bootstrap lower bound,
at least 70% precision among admitted departures with at least 20 admissions,
and no family median regression beyond 2%. Forecast support, identity, and
admission evidence must be complete. A fresh naturalistic confirmation must
then show non-inferiority before the default changes.

### Q2 — Decision-grade typed temporal answers

Observed gap: the engine returned and the agent preserved all 240 canonical
typed answers in the v0.6 TemporalBench run, but official choice accuracy was
28.3%, versus 30.4% for the direct-agent control. Preservation is complete;
answer usefulness is not yet demonstrated.

User hypothesis: supported typed answers should improve agent decisions, while
weak evidence should produce an explicit advisory or abstention rather than a
confident but unhelpful label.

Before implementation, freeze a property-stratified held-out corpus and score
engine completeness, exact agent preservation, support calibration, selective
accuracy/utility, and coverage separately. Promotion requires no loss of
canonical or relationship preservation, no immutable-primary change, no
support inflation, non-negative matched utility overall, and improvement in
at least one preregistered weak property without a property regression larger
than 2 percentage points. If confidence cannot discriminate errors, the
correct decision is calibrated abstention, not prompt coercion.

The corpus also includes the seasonal-trend controls preregistered in the
external-evaluation intake. Trend direction, numeric slope, uncertainty,
support, and automation eligibility must remain mutually consistent.

### Q3 — Context recall at fixed precision

Observed gap: the retained 112-case structural stress run had zero leakage and
100% empirical admission precision, but only about 46% recall. Naturalistic
compiler supply and engine admission are reported as separate stages.

User hypothesis: agents should discover more genuinely usable dated evidence;
humans should never pay for that yield with invented timing or magnitude.

Freeze new generator seeds and naturalistic documents before changing parsing
or compilation. Promotion requires 100% empirical admission precision, zero
leakage, exact primary immutability, exact typed-relationship preservation,
and a statistically positive recall increase on a matched fresh shard. Report
compiler proposal recall, engine contract completeness, scenario yield, and
automation-eligible yield separately. Scenario-only context may improve
explanation but may not be counted as an admitted numerical effect.

### Q4 — Stable context-mechanism selection

Observed gap: on the four-case CiK sensor confirmation Gnomon's mean RCRPS
improved because of one large win, while its median worsened and it lost three
of four cases. This is insufficient evidence for an automatic selector.

User hypothesis: repeated runs should select a mechanism for evidence visible
at decision time, not because one hindsight outlier dominates an average.

Freeze unseen seeds and multiple task families before modifying selection.
Promotion requires complete matched runs, lower mean and median proper score,
more wins than losses, a non-negative paired uncertainty bound, no family
median regression beyond 2%, and stable selection under registered seed or
sampling perturbations. Runtime features may describe only pre-cutoff evidence.
Automation remains false until this gate passes prospectively.

### Q5 — Crash-safe, economical orchestration

Observed gap: the 80-row Evidence run completed but used 317 model requests,
2.17 million prompt tokens, 353 transport attempts, and recovered from 16
row-level provider failures.

User hypothesis: agents should obtain the same governed answer with fewer
round trips and predictable recovery; humans should see cost, retries, and
fallbacks rather than unexplained latency.

Freeze a representative engine/agent shard with cold and warm receipt paths.
Promotion requires exact semantic and preservation parity, zero additional
leakage or fallback, 100% resume correctness under fault injection, at least
25% fewer model requests and prompt tokens on the matched shard, bounded
retries and timeouts, and no material p95 latency regression. Compiler,
product-tool, and final-agent calls are reported separately.

### Q6 — Regime-aware anomaly behavior

Reported agent feedback identified two potentially general defects: a detected
regime shift may be repeated as many post-shift anomalies, and an isolated
spike may contaminate the following prediction so a normal rebound is flagged.
Q6 is queued behind S1, S2, Q1, and Q2 unless current-head reproduction shows
an automation-eligible false alert. Its frozen reproduction and completion
gates live in `docs/v0.7-external-evaluation-intake.md`.

## Final release gate

After S1, S2, Q1–Q6 and every reproduced intake finding have a promote,
retained no-build, or retained non-reproduction decision, rerun focused tests
and fresh small matched ContextBench, TemporalBench, and CiK shards at the
exact clean candidate commit. Run the full local suite only after the small
shards are green. A release recommendation requires:

- all safety invariants exact;
- every row accounted for, including failures and abstentions;
- output, reasoning, and topology results reported separately;
- actual response inspection recorded;
- no public performance claim unsupported by a retained complete artifact;
- CI green on the exact proposed head.

All gates passed for the 0.7.0 runtime candidate. The exact product and
release-boundary head `fa59f2b` passed CI on Python 3.11, 3.12, and 3.13,
benchmark validation, package smoke, and the container build. The final
completion record is metadata-only and must receive the same exact-head
workflow confirmation before the annotated `v0.7.0` tag is created.
