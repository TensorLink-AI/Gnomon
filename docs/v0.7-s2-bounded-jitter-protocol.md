# v0.7 S2 bounded timestamp jitter protocol

Status: frozen before production implementation.

## User boundary

Ordinary scheduler and scrape jitter is an alignment defect, not a missing
measurement. An agent should be able to pass a slightly jittered operational
series through the default safe path without first writing a resampler. A
human must still be able to see exactly what moved, by how much, and which
grid Gnomon inferred. Neither interface may confuse alignment with an
invented value or silently merge two observations.

This protocol independently regenerates the external evaluator's reported
20-minute case. The evaluator's original fixture is not available. Fixture
identity and expected labels are not available to production code.

## Frozen policy

- `repair=off` remains strict and never moves timestamps.
- `repair=safe` may align timestamps only when every observation is within a
  bounded tolerance of one deterministic grid. It never fills a gap, resolves
  a value conflict, drops a point, or merges observations.
- `repair=aggressive` uses the same bounded alignment rule. Its existing,
  separately disclosed authority to interpolate small interior gaps and
  resolve exact-timestamp value conflicts is unchanged.
- For a fixed-duration cadence, alignment tolerance is 1% of the cadence,
  capped at 60 seconds. The boundary is inclusive. A 20-minute cadence
  therefore permits at most 12 seconds of displacement.
- Grid phase is inferred from the observations. It is not forced to a Unix,
  clock-hour, day, or aesthetically round boundary. Phase estimation first
  assigns provisional integer slots from the first ordered observation, then
  takes the median residual after removing those slot offsets.
- A cadence inferred from jitter may be rounded to a whole-minute schedule
  only when the inferred median step is itself within the frozen tolerance of
  that schedule. Otherwise the nearest representable whole-second cadence is
  considered. Exact regular grids continue through strict inference and are
  never restamped.
- Alignment is all-or-nothing per series. If any observation is outside the
  tolerance, if two observations map to one slot, or if slot order is not
  strictly increasing, no partial aligned series is emitted.
- The repair record exposes cadence, phase, tolerance, count, and maximum
  displacement as machine-readable values. Timestamp alignment may warn and
  degrade support, but it does not consume the ceiling reserved for filled,
  dropped, or conflict-resolved values.

Calendar-month alignment is outside this issue and remains governed by the
explicit `regrid=month_start` declaration. Calendar-aware DST behavior must
remain unchanged.

## Frozen cases

The benchmark covers:

1. an exact 20-minute grid at a seven-minute phase under off, safe, and
   aggressive repair;
2. one-second scheduler jitter affecting more than 30% of rows;
3. bounded scrape jitter up to the inclusive 12-second boundary;
4. a 12.001-second displacement immediately outside that boundary;
5. a phase-shifted grid that must not be epoch-restamped;
6. two observations that would collide in one aligned slot;
7. reordered bounded-jitter input, with reordering disclosed separately;
8. mixed 20-minute and 10-minute cadence;
9. bounded jitter plus one small real gap: safe refuses, while aggressive may
   interpolate and must disclose alignment and fill separately;
10. a long outage that remains a typed refusal even under aggressive repair.

Values and observation counts are compared before and after every accepted
alignment. Surface tests additionally cover direct runtime, inspect,
forecast, MCP tool execution, and CLI behavior.

## Promotion gates

- Exact grids pass without a repair action and retain their timestamps and
  values.
- Bounded jitter passes safe and aggressive while off remains strict.
- The learned phase, tolerance, count, and maximum displacement are visible
  and deterministic.
- More than 30% aligned timestamps do not trigger `EXCESSIVE_REPAIR` by
  themselves.
- The outside-boundary, collision, mixed-cadence, and long-outage cases fail
  with typed errors.
- No accepted alignment merges, drops, reorders, or changes a value.
- Reordering and gap filling, where applicable, have separate action codes
  and counts.
- Focused production tests and the complete local suite pass before release.
